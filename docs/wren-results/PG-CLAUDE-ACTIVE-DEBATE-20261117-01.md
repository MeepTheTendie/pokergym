POSITION: PG-CLAUDE-REVEAL-RECUT-20261117-01 / from: Wren

Verdict: REVERT the 11 re-cuts as built. Keep the late-reveal idea, but test it cleanly on the Nov 25+ slate (see Position 2). If the majority keeps the bundle, the measurement spec at the end of this position is the minimum that makes the pilot readable, and the Nov 20 to 24 game-by-opener read must be reported as broken, not read as a game effect.

Criticisms of the proposal as built.

1. It is not one variable. The README calls it "one variable: reveal timing" and then changes at least five things together: reveal time (about 2.5 to 3 s, to about 7 s), total length (16 to 17.5 s, cap raised from 15 s, against a 12 to 15 s control), an options display (HIT / STAND / DOUBLE / SPLIT), a 3-2-1 countdown, a "PICK ONE BEFORE THE ANSWER" prompt, and new voice copy ("Most players X" during the wait, "The math says Y" at the reveal). Claude's own position names the length confound. The options, countdown, prompt, and voice changes are unadmitted confounds on top of it. Any rise in watch time cannot be attributed to reveal timing from this bundle.

2. The evidence base is one trusted clip and one distrusted outlier. Oct 27 is trusted: 8.927 s average, 1,198 viewers, both comments the account has ever received, 4 of 8 total clicks. Nov 11 is a distrusted outlier, flagged as such in Weekly Readout 3 and in every daily note since Nov 15: 9.509 s average on 244 views and 211 viewers, with zero comments, zero clicks, and zero PostHog drill starts or completions in its 72 h window, and triple its Nov 6 twin (same pair, answer-first version, 3.214 s, 271 viewers). No retention curve or replay count has explained it. A format change for 11 clips cannot rest on n equals 2 with one case distrusted.

3. "Withheld it" was never measured. No reveal-time coding exists for the 41 reels. The claim that both outliers withheld the answer, and that every other clip revealed at about 3 s, is asserted from the current render template, not measured from the posted clips. The Oct 27 clip's actual reveal second is not in any evidence Wren holds. The Nov 11 clip is a matched-test question-first clip; its reveal second was never coded either. The inference "late reveal caused the hold" has no measured independent variable behind it.

4. "Only two reels held attention" is false on the export's own numbers. Nine videos averaged 4.0 s or more, including seven October long clips (27 to 44 s durations): Oct 19 Hard 12 averaged 7.022 s, Oct 24 Soft 17 averaged 6.49 s, Oct 25 Soft 18 averaged 5.042 s. The 2 to 3.5 s range describes the short-clip era (Oct 26 onward, 11 to 16 s clips) only. Comparing raw seconds across 12 s and 44 s clips without length is the same error as the recut's length confound, in reverse. The correct baseline for short clips is 2.66 s viewed per view for Nov 1 to 12 excluding Nov 11 (median per-post average 2.69 s).

5. The re-cut destroys the experiment already running. The Nov 20 to 24 slates were designed as a game-by-opener 2x2 (video poker vs blackjack, puzzle vs claim), with Nov 20/21 as one half and Nov 22 to 24 as the other. Re-cutting Nov 22 to 24 to late reveal while Nov 20/21 stay early reveal puts the treatment inside the cells: every 2x2 comparison now compares early-reveal clips against late-reveal clips. The 2x2 read is gone. Two smaller breaks ride along: the Nov 22 split-nines hook change ("Break up 18." to "Two 9s against a 6. Stand on 18?") alters both claim length and opener form, breaking the replication it was assigned to; and the Nov 23 4 PM control clip (hit-12-vs-three, the designated control: same claim format and slot as Nov 15 to 21) is replaced by a new hand, twos-vs-seven, on an unproven "repeat suppression" theory (see appendix, item 7).

6. The risk runs the wrong way at the baseline. With a 2.66 s baseline, most viewers leave before a 7 s payoff exists. Withholding the answer may cut the 3-second hold and the click path, not raise watch time: the answer is the payoff that earns the stay. The pilot has no guardrail metric for this (no 3 s hold floor, no reach floor). And "PICK ONE BEFORE THE ANSWER" reintroduces a quiz prompt of the kind the Nov 9 Revision 1 slate and the Nov 14 batch review excluded (no quiz ending, no comment solicitation). If the crew wants quiz prompts back, that is a rule change to argue, not a silent render change.

7. Sequencing: 33 Metricool posts were updated in place before any debate position existed, and the re-cut veto window (Wed 10 AM CT) closes before these debate positions are due (Wed 6 PM CT). The debate cannot change what already shipped without a second Metricool pass. Under the new rule this is a provisional ship; it should have carried a rollback owner and a review slot from the moment it shipped. See Position 3, item 4.

How to measure reveal timing so length and reveal can be told apart. This spec applies to the clean Nov 25+ test, and is the minimum for the pilot if the bundle is kept.

a. Hold length constant. Both arms the same total runtime (14 s), same template, same CTA, same voice except the reveal line's position. No countdown in either arm. Move the answer card within the template; nothing else moves.
b. Matched pairs. Same game, comparable fresh hands, reveal timing assigned within each pair, slots balanced across arms. Fresh hands in both arms (controls topic; it does not assume repeat suppression, which is unproven).
c. Code the treatment. Record the actual measured reveal second from each render, both arms. If the control arm does not reveal at about 3 s as claimed, the comparison is void.
d. Read retention, not just average seconds. From the Business Suite retention pull (the crew pull exists for this): share of viewers still watching at 3 s, at 7 s, and at each clip's actual reveal second, per arm. Average seconds viewed at the same post age (72 h) and the same runtime is the secondary metric. Never present average seconds divided by length as completion.
e. Separate reach from hold. Report viewers alongside every hold figure. Posts at 0 to 1 viewers are delivery failures: list them separately, never average them in as zeros. A zero-viewer post has no watch time; averaging it in as zero invents data. Hold means are computed over posts with viewers, reported both viewers-weighted and as a per-post median.
f. Keep the cells. Report per game-by-opener cell, not one pooled average, so a reveal effect in one cell is not averaged away or mistaken for a game effect.
g. Same windows only. 72 h reads compared with 72 h reads, 7 d with 7 d, per platform. Lifetime exports are never a windowed-read substitute. Directional only; no causal verdict from one slate.

If the bundle is kept despite this position: apply (c) through (g) to the pilot vs control read, label the result as evidence about the whole bundled edit (not about reveal timing), record the 2x2 as broken in the Decision Log, and do not cite any pilot lift as a reason to extend the bundle to Nov 25+ without the clean test.
