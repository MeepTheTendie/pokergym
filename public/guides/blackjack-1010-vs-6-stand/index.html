10-10 against a 6: split or stand?

The answer

Stand. Never split tens, whatever the dealer shows.

Play this exact hand →The coach grades your call and explains it. Free, no signup.

Why

Twenty beats every dealer total except 21, and a dealer 6 busts about 42% of the time. Splitting gives that away.

Each split hand starts from a single ten and has to be built again, with a second bet on the table. For 10-10 against a 6, the trainer’s engine puts standing at about +0.70 per unit bet and splitting, both hands counted, at about +0.57. Against every other upcard the split is further behind.

The same goes for any two ten-value cards, such as king-queen: stand on 20.

Rules this answer assumes

Six decks, dealer stands on soft 17, double on any first two cards, double after split allowed, no surrender, and the dealer checks for blackjack before you act, so a dealer blackjack never takes a double or split bet. These are the rules the free trainer deals and grades with.

What changes the answer

  • Dealer hits or stands on soft 17, any number of decks, double after split or not: basic strategy stands on 10-10 against every upcard.
  • Surrender never applies: 20 is far too strong to give up half the bet.
  • Card counting is the only exception. With the Hi-Lo count, splitting 10s against a 6 becomes correct at a true count of about +4, and against a 5 at about +5. Without a count, never split them.

Questions

Is splitting tens ever correct?

Only for card counters at high positive counts (a Hi-Lo true count of about +4 against a 6, +5 against a 5). Basic strategy never splits tens.

What if the dealer shows a 6?

Stand. A 6 is one of the dealer’s two weakest cards, and 20 is already the winner most of the time.

Can you split a king and a queen?

Many casinos let you split any two ten-value cards, but you shouldn’t. King-queen is a hard 20, so stand.
