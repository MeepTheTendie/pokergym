15 against a dealer 10: hit or stand?

The answer

Hit. Hitting 15 against a 10 loses about 50 cents per dollar; standing loses about 54.

Why

A dealer 10 makes 17 or better about 79% of the time and busts only about 21%. Standing on 15 means you win only when the dealer busts, so standing is a losing play by construction — the coach scores it at about −0.54 per dollar.

Hitting is only a little better, and it looks worse than it is. A 15 busts on any card of seven or higher, which is about 57% of the cards left in the shoe. But the other 43% produce 17 to 21, and that is enough to move the number to about −0.50.

Doubling is never right here. It is the same card you would draw anyway, with twice the money on a hand that is behind, so the coach puts it near −1.01 per dollar. The choice is hit or stand, and the answer is hit at every count you will meet.

Questions

Should you hit on 15 in blackjack?

Against a 10, a 9 and a 7 through ace, yes. Against a 2 through 6, stand: the dealer is likely to bust and your 15 already beats a bust.

Is 15 vs 10 a surrender hand?

Not in most rule sets. Surrender is usually offered only against 9, 10 and ace on the first two cards, and 15 vs 10 is one of the hands where surrendering is correct when it is available. This trainer uses rules with no surrender.

Why is hitting 15 better than standing if I bust more than half the time?

Because standing wins only on a dealer bust, which happens about 21% of the time. Hitting wins that 21% plus the hands where you improve to 17 to 21 and the dealer does not beat you.
