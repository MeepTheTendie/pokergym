16 against a dealer 10: hit or stand?

The answer

Hit. If the table offers surrender, surrender instead; standing on 16 against a 10 is the weaker play.

Why

Standing on 16 only wins when the dealer busts, and a dealer 10 busts only about 23% of the time (six decks, after the dealer has checked for blackjack). It makes 20 about 37% of the time.

Hitting gives you a chance to make 17 to 21. Some of those hands still lose, but the alternative loses more often. For 10-6 from a full six-deck shoe, the trainer’s engine puts hitting at about −0.53 per unit bet and standing at about −0.54. The gap is small, which is why the hand feels like a coin flip, but hitting is the better play.

Rules this answer assumes

Six decks, blackjack pays 3:2, dealer stands on soft 17, double on any first two cards, double after split allowed, split up to four hands, split aces get one card each and can’t be resplit, no surrender, and the dealer checks for blackjack before you act, so a dealer blackjack never takes a double or split bet. These are the rules the free trainer deals and grades with.

What changes the answer

  • Surrender. If late surrender is offered, surrender 16 against a 9, 10 or ace (and 15 against a 10). You keep half your bet, a loss of 50 cents per dollar, which beats the roughly 53 cents that hitting loses here.
  • Dealer hits or stands on soft 17, four decks or eight: without surrender, two-card 16 against a 10 stays a hit.
  • A pair of 8s. If your 16 is 8-8, split it instead. Basic strategy splits 8s against every upcard, including a 10: 8-8 against a 10.
  • Card counting. With the Hi-Lo count, players stand on 16 against a 10 when the true count is zero or higher. It is the best-known count-based change in the game. Basic strategy assumes you are not counting, so it hits.

Questions

Should you ever stand on 16 against a 10?

Not in basic strategy. If surrender is offered, surrender; otherwise hit. Only card counters stand, at a positive or zero Hi-Lo true count.

Is it better to surrender 16 against a 10?

Yes, when late surrender is allowed. Surrender loses exactly half the bet; hitting loses about 53 cents per dollar in the trainer’s six-deck game.

Does the count change the 16 decision?

Yes. With the Hi-Lo count, stand on 16 against a 10 when the true count is zero or higher. Basic strategy assumes no count and says hit.
