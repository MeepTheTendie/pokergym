Should you split a pair of 2s? The 2-2 rule

The answer

Split 2s against a 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 or 7. Hit against an 8, 9, 10 or ace.

Why

A pair of twos is a hard 4, the worst total on the table: it cannot win without three more good cards. Splitting gives you two hands that each start at 2 and can still become anything, and against a weak dealer card that option is worth paying for. Against a 6 the coach rates splitting at about +0.24 per dollar and hitting at about +0.02.

The rule stops at 8 because the dealer stops being weak. Against a 9, hitting is about −0.24 and splitting about −0.36: you are putting a second bet behind a hand that needs three cards to improve, while the dealer is already showing the card that makes 19 about 35% of the time.

Splitting 2s also doubles your exposure, which is why it only appears against 2 through 7. Against 8 and above you are paying twice to be behind, and the correct play is to hit the hard 4 and accept the loss.

Questions

Do you split 2s against a 2?

Yes. Splitting 2s against a 2 is correct even though the dealer is showing the same card, because a hard 4 is worse than two hands starting at 2.

Do you split 2s against an 8?

No. Against 8, 9, 10 and ace you hit. The dealer is too strong for a second bet on a hand that starts at 2.

Do you split 2s against a 5 or a 6?

Yes, and those are the best cards for it. Against a 6 splitting is worth about 24 cents per dollar against 2 cents for hitting.
