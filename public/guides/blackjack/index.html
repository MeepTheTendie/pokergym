Hit or stand? Look beyond your total.

Choose your move before revealing each answer. These examples start with a fresh six-deck shoe, with only your two cards and the dealer’s upcard known.

Poker Gym’s dealer stands on all 17s, including soft 17. Doubling is available on these two-card hands; surrender is unavailable. The examples compare the simulator’s hit, stand and double choices. Different rules or a depleted shoe can change a decision.

1. Hard 16 against a six

10♠ 6♥

Dealer shows 6♦. Hit, stand or double?

Reveal the decision and reason

Stand. Your total is uncomfortable, but taking a card can bust your hand immediately. The dealer must draw below 17 and can bust too. In this example, the coach ranks standing above hitting and doubling. Standing is still a losing proposition on average; it simply has the best average result of the available choices.

2. The same hard 16 against a ten

10♠ 6♥

Dealer shows 10♦. Does the same total mean the same move?

Reveal the decision and reason

Hit. The dealer’s stronger upcard changes the comparison. Standing on 16 loses against any dealer finish from 17 through 21. Hitting risks a bust, but in this fresh-shoe example it has a slightly better average result than standing. Both choices lose on average. Surrender is not an option in this simulator.

3. Soft 18 against a nine

A♠ 7♥

Dealer shows 9♦. Is 18 always enough to stand?

Reveal the decision and reason

Hit. This is a soft 18: the ace currently counts as 11, but can become 1. One hit cannot bust this two-card hand. For example, drawing a ten turns it into hard 18 rather than 28. Against the nine, the chance to improve makes hitting the coach’s preferred available move here. Reassess your total and choices after the next card.

A good decision can still lose

The coach compares average outcomes under the simulator’s model, not the next card. Its hit search uses fixed remaining-rank weights and a bounded continuation search.
