Soft 16 (A-5): hit, double or stand?

The answer

Double against a 4, 5 or 6. Hit against a 2, 3, 7, 8, 9, 10 or ace.

Why

Soft 16 cannot bust. The ace drops to one, so the worst total you can reach is 6, and that is what makes doubling safe with an unmade hand. Against a 6 the coach rates doubling at about +0.19 per dollar, hitting at about +0.10 and standing at about −0.15.

The double needs a weak dealer card to pay. A dealer 6 busts about 42% of the time, so the extra bet is a bet on the dealer failing. Against a 2 or a 3 that bust rate falls to about 35% and 37%, and the double stops being right: against those cards you hit and keep the option.

From a 7 upward you hit, and you never stand. Standing on soft 16 throws away the ace entirely for a total that only wins on a bust, and against a 9 or better even hitting is a losing play you have to make anyway.

Questions

Is A-5 a soft 16?

Yes. The ace counts as 11 gives 16, and it can drop to 1 for 6, so the hand is soft and cannot bust on the next card.

Do you double a soft 16 against a 6?

Yes, when doubling is allowed. Against 4, 5 and 6 the double is clearly better than hitting; against 2 and 3 you hit instead.

What is the difference between soft 16 and hard 16?

Hard 16 cannot take a card without risking a bust and is the worst hand in the game. Soft 16 can take any card safely, which is why one is a double and the other is a reluctant hit.
