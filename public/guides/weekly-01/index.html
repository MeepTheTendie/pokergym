1. Soft 18 against a ten

A♠ 7♥

Dealer shows 10♦. Choose your move before revealing.

Reveal the decision and reason

Hit. The ace can switch from 11 to 1, so one hit cannot bust this two-card hand. Against the ten, the coach prefers trying to improve over standing on 18. Reassess after the next card.

2. Soft 19 against a four

A♠ 8♥

Dealer shows 4♦. Choose your move before revealing.

Reveal the decision and reason

Stand. A soft total does not automatically mean hit or double. Here, keeping 19 ranks above doubling and hitting under the simulator’s stand-on-soft-17 rules.

3. Eights against a nine

8♠ 8♥

Dealer shows 9♦. Choose your move before revealing.

Reveal the decision and reason

Split. Two eights make hard 16. Splitting gives each eight a new starting hand. The coach ranks it above hit, stand and double here, although every available choice still loses on average.

4. Nines against a seven

9♠ 9♥

Dealer shows 7♦. Choose your move before revealing.

Reveal the decision and reason

Stand. Having a pair does not automatically mean split. Keeping 18 against the seven has a better average outcome in this model than turning it into two nine-starting hands.

5. Hard nine against a two

6♠ 3♥

Dealer shows 2♦. Choose your move before revealing.

Reveal the decision and reason

Hit. Doubling is tempting on nine, but the dealer’s two matters. In this model hitting narrowly outranks doubling. Unlike doubling, hitting leaves further decisions available.
