11 vs ace in blackjack: double or hit? (2026)

Hit. Double 11 against 2 through 10, but against an ace just hit.

Eleven is the best doubling total in the game because any ten-value card makes 21. But a double only pays if the dealer is likely to pay you off, and an ace upcard is the least likely card to bust: about 12% of the time, against 21% for a 10 and 42% for a 6.

The ace also finishes on 21 about 36% of the time, the highest of any upcard. Against that, committing two units to a hand that needs a ten just to reach 21 is the wrong price. The coach rates hitting at about +0.15 per dollar and doubling at about +0.13.

That gap is small, so doubling against an ace is a cheap mistake rather than a disaster. Against a 10 the same double is worth about +0.18 against +0.12 for hitting, which is why 10 is a double and the ace is not — the difference is the dealer’s bust rate, not your hand.

Questions

Should you double 11 against an ace?

No. Hit. The gap is only about 2 cents per dollar, but the double is the wrong side of it because an ace upcard busts less often than any other card.

Why is 11 vs 10 a double but 11 vs ace a hit?

Because the dealer’s bust rate falls from about 21% to about 12%. You are betting on the dealer failing, and an ace rarely fails.

Does the answer change if the dealer hits soft 17?

Not for 11 against an ace. It stays a hit in the common rule sets, including the stands-on-all-17s rules this trainer uses.
