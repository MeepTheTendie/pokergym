Jacks or Better: 4 to a flush or a low pair?

The answer

Hold the four to a flush. A low pair pays nothing until it improves, so the flush draw beats it.

Why

In the deal K-Q-7-5 of clubs with a second 5, holding the four clubs returns about 1.28 per coin and holding the pair of 5s about 0.82. Four to a flush completes roughly one time in five, and along the way it also pays whenever you pair a high card.

The low pair is the problem, not the flush draw. A pair of 5s is worth nothing as it stands; it needs to become two pair, trips or better before it pays a coin. You are comparing a draw that pays about a fifth of the time with a made hand that pays never.

This is the exact opposite of the high-pair decision. A pair of jacks or better already pays even money, so it beats the same four-card flush draw. Rank is the whole difference between the two answers, which is why the two hands are easy to confuse.

Questions

Do you keep four to a flush or a low pair?

Keep the four to a flush in 9/6 Jacks or Better. It returns about 1.28 per coin against about 0.82 for a low pair.

What if the pair is jacks or better?

Then keep the pair. A paying high pair already returns even money and beats four to a flush.

Does it matter which cards make the flush draw?

No. Four cards of the same suit is four to a flush whatever the ranks, and it outranks a low pair either way.
