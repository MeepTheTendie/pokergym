Odds of losing 6 baccarat hands in a row (2026)

About 1.4%, or roughly one time in 69 decided hands, if you bet Banker on every hand.

Start with one hand. Banker wins about 45.9% of the time, Player about 44.6%, and about 9.5% of hands are ties. A tie returns your stake, so it is neither a win nor a loss and does not break a losing run. Throwing ties out, Banker loses about 49.3% of decided hands.

Six losses in a row is then 0.4932 to the sixth power, which is 0.0144 — 1.44%. Losing runs are not a warning sign, they are the arithmetic: across 100 hands you should see at least one run of six Banker losses about three times in four.

That is why a martingale fails. Doubling after every loss to win one unit back means surviving six losses needs 1+2+4+8+16+32 = 63 units already committed, with 64 more to place. Table limits and bankroll run out before the sequence does, and the 1.06% edge is charged on every one of those bets.

Questions

What are the odds of losing 6 baccarat hands in a row?

About 1.4%, roughly 1 in 69 decided hands, betting Banker every time. Betting Player it is slightly worse at about 1.5%, because Player wins less often than Banker.

Do ties count as losses in a losing streak?

No. A tie pushes and returns your stake, so a run of six losses means six hands you actually lost, not six hands you did not win. Counting ties as losses makes the streak look rarer than it is.

Does losing 6 in a row mean the next hand is more likely to win?

No. Baccarat hands are close to independent, so the next hand is still about 45.9% for Banker. Past results do not change the tableau or the shoe composition in any way you can use.
