Soft 17 (A-6) against a dealer 2: hit, double or stand?

The answer

Hit. Never stand on soft 17, and against a 2 hitting edges out doubling.

Play this exact hand →The coach grades your call and explains it. Free, no signup.

Why

Seventeen only wins when the dealer busts, and a dealer 2 busts about 35% of the time. Standing on soft 17 here loses roughly 15 cents per dollar in the coach’s six-deck, stands-on-soft-17 game.

The ace means you cannot bust on the next card, so hitting is free improvement: it brings the hand to about break-even. Doubling is almost as good (about half a cent worse), which is why some charts list double against a 2 under hits-soft-17 rules; against 3 through 6 the double becomes the clear play.

Questions

Should you ever stand on soft 17?

No. Soft 17 is never a stand in basic strategy. Hit it against 2, 7, 8, 9, 10 and ace, and double it against 3 through 6 when doubling is allowed.

Do you double soft 17 against a 2?

Under the standard stands-on-soft-17 rules, no: hitting is slightly better. Some hits-soft-17 charts double it against a 2; the difference is under one cent per dollar.

Why is soft 17 a weak hand?

Seventeen beats nothing on its own; it only wins if the dealer busts or pushes with 17. Because the ace counts as 1 or 11, you can take a card with no risk of busting.
