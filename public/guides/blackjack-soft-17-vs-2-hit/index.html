Soft 17 (A-6) against a dealer 2: hit, double or stand?

The answer

Hit. Never stand on soft 17, and against a 2 hitting edges out doubling.

Play this exact hand →The coach grades your call and explains it. Free, no signup.

Why

Seventeen only wins when the dealer busts, and a dealer 2 busts about 35% of the time. Standing on soft 17 here loses roughly 15 cents per dollar in the coach’s six-deck, stands-on-soft-17 game.

The ace means you cannot bust on the next card, so hitting is free improvement: it brings the hand to about break-even. Doubling is almost as good (about half a cent worse), which is why single-deck charts list a double against a 2; against 3 through 6 the double becomes the clear play.

Rules this answer assumes

Six decks, blackjack pays 3:2, dealer stands on soft 17, double on any first two cards, double after split allowed, split up to four hands, split aces get one card each and can’t be resplit, no surrender, and the dealer checks for blackjack before you act, so a dealer blackjack never takes a double or split bet. These are the rules the free trainer deals and grades with.

What changes the answer

  • Number of decks. In a single-deck game the same coach engine flips this hand to a double against a 2 (about +0.015 against +0.009 for hitting). With two, six or eight decks, hitting stays ahead.
  • Dealer hits soft 17. Standard six- and eight-deck charts still hit soft 17 against a 2 and double it against 3 through 6. The hand that switches to a double against a 2 under that rule is soft 18 (A-7).
  • Doubling not allowed. If you can’t double (a three-card soft 17 like A-2-4, or a table that limits doubles), hit soft 17 against every upcard. Never stand.
  • After a split. With double after split (the trainer’s rule), a soft 17 you make after splitting (6s that draw an ace, say) plays the same way. Without it, hit. Split aces are the exception: they get one card and stand.
  • Blackjack pays 6:5 instead of 3:2. No change. The payout only touches two-card naturals, and soft 17 isn’t one.

Questions

Should you ever stand on soft 17?

No. Soft 17 is never a stand in basic strategy. Hit it against 2, 7, 8, 9, 10 and ace, and double it against 3 through 6 when doubling is allowed.

Do you double soft 17 against a 2?

In a six- or eight-deck game, no: hitting is slightly better. Single-deck charts double it against a 2; either way the difference is under one cent per dollar.

Why is soft 17 a weak hand?

Seventeen beats nothing on its own; it only wins if the dealer busts or pushes with 17. Because the ace counts as 1 or 11, you can take a card with no risk of busting.
