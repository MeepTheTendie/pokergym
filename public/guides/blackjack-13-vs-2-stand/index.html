13 against a dealer 2: hit or stand?

The answer

Stand. Thirteen against a 2 is a stand, while 12 against a 2 is a hit.

Why

Both hands win only when the dealer busts, and a dealer 2 busts about 35% of the time. What separates them is what you can draw without busting. A 12 busts on ten-value cards only, five ranks out of thirteen. A 13 busts on a 9 as well as every ten-value card, six ranks.

That single extra bust rank is enough to flip the decision. The coach rates standing on 13 against a 2 at about −0.29 and hitting at about −0.31, so standing is better by roughly 1.4 cents per dollar — one of the smallest edges in basic strategy.

Against a 3 the same stand is better still, because a 3 busts slightly more often. Against a 7 or higher the answer reverses and you hit, because the dealer is far more likely to make a pat hand than to bust.

Questions

Do you hit 13 against a 2?

No. Stand on 13 against a 2 and a 3. Hit it against 4 through 6 you stand as well, and hit from 7 upward.

Why is 12 vs 2 a hit but 13 vs 2 a stand?

A 12 busts only on a ten-value card. A 13 also busts on a 9, so there are more ways to ruin the hand and fewer good cards to draw. Standing becomes the better losing option.

Is standing on 13 vs 2 a mistake?

No, though the margin is small. Hitting is only about 1.4 cents per dollar worse, which is why players who hit it do not lose much — but they do lose.
