Which five cards actually count?

In Hold’em, your best hand uses exactly five of the seven cards available to you. You can use both private cards, one, or neither. Choose your five before opening each answer.

These puzzles teach hand evaluation. Poker Gym’s Hold’em gym grades your selection of five cards; it does not simulate opponents, betting decisions or a complete poker strategy.

1. Do you have to use your private cards?

Your private cards:

2♥ 3♦

Shared board:

10♠ J♠ Q♠ K♠ A♠

Which five cards make your best hand?

Reveal the best five and reason

Use all five board cards: 10♠ J♠ Q♠ K♠ A♠. That is a royal flush. Including either private card makes the hand weaker. Every remaining player can use this same board, so every remaining player has the same best hand. Having an ace in your private cards would not break that tie.

2. Two pair needs one kicker

Your private cards:

A♠ Q♥

Shared board:

K♠ K♥ 7♦ 7♣ 2♦

You have two pair. Does your queen count too?

Reveal the best five and reason

K♠ K♥ 7♦ 7♣ A♠. The two pairs take four places. Only one place remains, and your ace is the highest available kicker. Your queen is the sixth card and contributes nothing to this five-card hand. Another player with an ace can tie this hand even if their other private card is lower than your queen; another player might also make a stronger hand, such as a full house.

3. An ace can be low

Your private cards:

A♠ 2♥

Shared board:

3♦ 4♣ 5♠ K♥ K♦

Keep the pair of kings, or use a straight?

Reveal the best five and reason

A♠ 2♥ 3♦ 4♣ 5♠. This is a five-high straight, often called a wheel. A straight beats one pair, so the kings are left out. The ace acts as 1 here; it does not make this an ace-high straight. A six-high straight would beat it. An ace can be low in A–2–3–4–5 or high in 10–J–Q–K–A; a straight cannot wrap around from king to ace to two.

Check strength, not the exact card identity

Sometimes more than one selection makes an equally strong best hand. Poker Gym accepts every selection that ties the strongest five-card result. Compare the hand category first, then the ranks that break ties. Suit alone does not break a tie.
